Why is that using my CPU?

Some things on a Mac are pegged but can't be quit — the window compositor (WindowServer) is the classic one. It has almost no cost of its own; it burns CPU drawing what other apps repaint. Sweep used to shrug and say "system process, leave it alone". Now it names the culprits:

WindowServer — 86% · The compositor doesn't burn CPU on its own, it burns it drawing what other apps repaint. Something is repainting hard.

• WhatsApp — 28% CPU

🛠 New Tune-ups tab

A third view beside Memory and CPU, for the waste that isn't a process you can quit — settings that cost CPU and GPU every second of every day:

  • Scaled displays. macOS shows you a flattering "looks like 3200 × 1800". What it actually does is render 6400 × 3600 and shrink it to fit your panel — 2.78× more pixels than necessary, on every frame, forever. Sweep is the only thing that tells you this, with the real numbers for your screens.
  • Transparency and motion effects — blur is recomputed per frame, on every display. One click each.
  • Separate Spaces per display — a documented cost on multi-monitor Macs.
  • Stuck settings panes — a System Settings panel left open can quietly spin at 30% CPU.
  • Leftover language servers — uninstalling an editor extension does not stop the server it started. Sweep tells you the fix that actually works (reload the editor window) instead of offering a button that would just make it respawn.

Backups are protected

A backup running in the background looks exactly like something you'd want to close: lots of memory, lots of CPU, no window. Sweep now recognises backup and sync engines — Syncovery, Carbon Copy Cloner, Arq, ChronoSync, Backblaze, SuperDuper, Retrospect, restic, Resilio, Syncthing and more — and will never suggest quitting one, least of all mid-backup.

Plain-language health

The header now says what the numbers mean, not just what they are — a huge load average alongside an idle CPU isn't a CPU shortage, and compressed memory is the real warning sign long before anything starts swapping.
