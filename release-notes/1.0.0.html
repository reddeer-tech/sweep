What's New in 1.0.0

Uninstall apps completely

A new Uninstall mode lists every app with its size and when you last opened it. Pick one and Sweep shows everything it spread around your Mac, its caches, preferences, containers, logs, launch agents and daemons, helper tools, login items, installer receipts and Dock icon, then removes it all together, to the Trash wherever it can. Apple's apps and anything macOS needs are never offered, anything still running or shared with another app is left unticked, and files owned by the system are removed after one administrator prompt

Apps you already removed that left files behind are listed too. Your own files that Sweep moves aside can be put back from the Uninstall screen, system files it removed are listed there with where they went

When you drag an app to the Trash, Sweep can tell you what it left behind, turn it on in Settings

Startup items

Optimize ▸ Startup lists every launch agent and daemon on your Mac, running or not, with who made it, when it was installed and whether it is on. You can switch third-party ones off and back on, or hand the app that owns one to Uninstall. Apple's services, Homebrew's and backup apps are never switched off

More to clean

  • Caches inside sandboxed apps
  • Old log folders from other apps, backup apps' logs are always kept
  • Junk on external drives: .DS_Store, ._ files, Spotlight and Trash folders
  • Old Downloads, Large & old files and Duplicates, lists for you to go through, never pre-selected, and anything you remove goes to the Trash
  • Duplicates can keep the newest or the oldest copy of each set in one click, and Sweep never lets you tick every copy
  • Finder ▸ Services ▸ Find Duplicates with Sweep searches a folder you pick in Finder, after asking you first
  • Links to each browser's own clear-history page and to Photos' duplicate finder, Sweep never touches your passwords, cookies or history itself
  • A tune-up that turns on Finder's own "Remove items from the Trash after 30 days"
  • Settings ▸ Scanning lets you leave out Old Downloads, external drives or system logs, and set the size for Large & old files

Quick Clean, alerts and Settings

  • Quick Clean in the menu bar scans and selects only pure caches, then shows you the review page, nothing is removed until you confirm there
  • Optional alerts when your disk is running low or clutter builds up
  • A Settings page for these, for starting at login and for keeping Sweep in the menu bar, all of them off until you turn them on

Monitor and Statistics

Optimize ▸ Monitor shows CPU, memory, GPU, battery health, temperatures, fan speeds, how much power your Mac is using, and a This Mac card with the model, chip, memory and macOS version. The battery card links to the charge history in System Settings. The menu bar can show the main readings live

Statistics shows how much space Sweep has reclaimed, and roughly how many photos, songs or hours of video that is, worked out on your Mac from its own log, nothing is sent anywhere

Health

A new Health tab in Optimize shows problems that already happened or are building up:

  • the Mac restarting because it stopped responding, and which apps were using the most memory when it ran short
  • background services that crash and restart over and over
  • log files that keep growing
  • apps whose memory only goes up, press "Watch for leaks" and Sweep measures it for three minutes

Crash reports from the last 30 days are now kept by Clean by default, because Health reads them

Optimize knows what belongs to macOS again

On macOS 27 Apple renamed the certificate it signs its own software with, so Sweep stopped recognising Apple's processes and offered to quit the Dock and to stop Apple's background services, behind a confirmation but offered all the same. Sweep now asks macOS itself through the system's own signature check, and if it can't confirm that Finder and WindowServer are Apple's it offers no actions at all and says why

Quit means quit, nothing more

When you quit an app from Optimize and it asks about unsaved work, cancelling now leaves the app open, before, a cancelled quit was followed by a signal that closed it anyway. Sweep never quits a backup app mid-backup and never stops a process that has changed since the scan. A background process is only called left behind when it's yours, nothing manages it and whatever started it has gone

Everything Sweep disables is listed under "Disabled by Sweep" with a button to turn it back on

Also

  • Memory used is reported the way Activity Monitor does it, file cache no longer counts as used
  • Sweep can no longer be started with injected code, which would have inherited its Full Disk Access
  • Programs Sweep runs for you, such as Homebrew, Docker and your shell, no longer get Sweep's Full Disk Access, only the few macOS tools that need it to size and remove files keep it
  • The switches used to test Sweep during development are no longer built into the app you download
  • File names are always shown as plain text
