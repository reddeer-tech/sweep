What's New

"Freed 0 B" now tells you why, correctly

When a clean removes gigabytes and your free space doesn't move, the usual cause on macOS is that local Time Machine snapshots still reference the blocks you just deleted. The files are gone; the space is held.

Sweep already measured that, and then led its explanation with a different theory — copy-on-write sharing — mentioning snapshots only as an afterthought. That was backwards. Purgeable space is measurable, so it is now diagnosed rather than guessed: if snapshots are holding the space, Sweep says so, gives you the figure, and points you at the section that releases it.

Copy-on-write sharing is still explained when it is genuinely the cause.

Why this matters

Deleting Xcode's unbootable test device clones on the machine this was built on removed 171 GB of files and moved free space by nothing at all. Both effects were real, and only one of them was the app's fault to explain. A cleaner that removes the right things but reports them wrongly is still a cleaner you can't trust.
